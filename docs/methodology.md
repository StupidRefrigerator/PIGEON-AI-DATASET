# UrbanGap methodology

## Scope and approved geographic decisions

The prediction region is established urban Greater Sydney. Original ABS 2021 SA1 geometries and Census G01 population are used; selected growth suburbs are excluded using ABS 2026 SAL polygons. Regional filtering uses the 2021 Greater Sydney SA1 attribute, while suburb membership uses representative points in the 2026 Greater Sydney boundary.

**Historical discrepancy:** independently rebuilding the saved density stage gives 11,612 SA1s at 50 people/km² and 11,138 at 500. At 50, the geographic union has 68 components and the former positional selections `[8, 47, 28, 38, 39]` reproduce the saved 10,753 preliminary SA1s exactly after 500 m expansion. At 500, there are 119 components and those positions produce only 23 SA1s. Thus the approved boundary was not generated by the assumed 500 recipe. The original notebook's source, stored output and subsequent execution state disagreed.

**Production resolution:** `study.DENSITY_THRESHOLD = 500` is the documented urban-core threshold. Approved preliminary membership is an explicit, versioned geographic selection input in `approved_preliminary_sa1_ids.txt`, independent of geometry-component enumeration. The pipeline reconstructs those complete polygons from raw SA1s, recomputes population/density, and recalculates growth exclusion using representative points. The 20 growth-suburb names are recorded in `study_selection.json`; all must match. Exactly 250 SA1s are excluded, yielding the approved 10,503 final IDs. Those IDs are checked against an independent final registry.

The registry preserves a human geographic decision, like a study-region mask. It is not a claim that 500-density connected components reproduce the historical region, nor an automatic selection rule for new boundary vintages. Revising this decision or using another geographic vintage requires a new approved selection. Historical anchors are retained for explanation only, never for positional selection.

## H3 grid and placement proxy

The final SA1 union is transformed to EPSG:4326 and passed to H3 resolution 9. Standard centre-based inclusion produces exactly 16,228 cells. Full cells remain uncut, so the union differs from the SA1 study footprint. H3 boundaries are converted from latitude/longitude order to Shapely longitude/latitude, then projected to EPSG:7856. IDs are sorted for deterministic exports.

National toilet records with valid coordinates are assigned directly to H3 cells and counted, then left-joined to every prediction cell. No historical Sydney-subset CSV is used. There are 2,570 mapped facilities in 1,911 prediction cells. Counts represent facilities, not cubicles, operating hours, access quality or optimal provision. They remain a placement proxy; zero does not prove unmet demand.

## Area-weighted population

For a destination zone Z and original SA1 S, the contribution is:

`population(S) * area(S intersect Z) / area(complete original S)`.

All metric area/distance operations use EPSG:7856. Relevant SA1s are selected by intersection, without clipping their original geometry before denominator calculation. Missing contextual population and non-positive denominator areas fail validation. Fractions remain unrounded. Population density is estimated residents divided by complete hexagon area in km².

For centre buffers, original SA1s are selected against the grid union buffered by 500 m, including SA1s outside the prediction boundary. GeoPandas `.buffer` creates 250 m and 500 m zones (16 segments per quadrant). Uniform resident distribution within each SA1 is an approximation. Buffer populations overlap and must not be summed as unique regional residents. The 2021 population does not describe daytime visits or contemporary migration.

## OSM extraction and counts

See `data_provenance.md` for exact commands. The NSW PBF is extracted with bbox `(150.45, -34.25, 151.40, -33.35)` and osmium `complete_ways`. Tag filters preserve shops, cafes/restaurants, bus stops, stations and parks, with referenced objects. GDAL converts PBF layers to GeoPackage. All points and multipolygons are read; incidental referenced/tagged objects remain as in the baseline.

Dedicated fields are supplemented by parsing GDAL `other_tags` with the original hstore regular expression. Polygon objects become representative points **before** projection, preserving the original method. Points and polygon representatives are combined. No name/proximity deduplication occurs; distinct OSM objects remain distinct.

Features use `shop` present except `vacant`; `amenity` cafe/restaurant; `highway=bus_stop`; `railway=station`. Other food types are not added. Category overlap is allowed. Objects intersecting a 500 m buffer of the complete grid union provide context. Inside counts use point `within` full hexagons; 250/500 m centre counts use `intersects` GeoPandas buffers. Edge predicates are intentionally preserved.

## Park footprint areas

Use original multipolygons with `leisure=park`. Exclude missing/empty geometries, repair validity, and fail if repair leaves unsupported non-polygon geometry. The original data have 6,998 park polygons; 6,493 intersect the grid's 500 m context. Their union prevents overlapping polygons from double-counting area.

Intersect that union with full hexagons and 250/500 m centre buffers. Park buffers use Shapely's original default of 8 segments per quadrant; this differs from the population/count approximation. This historical difference is retained to preserve every park area value. Old park representative-point counts are historical fields, excluded from the final schema.

## Nearest mapped park and coverage

Use `sjoin_nearest` and its spatial index over **all 6,998 available park polygons**, then collapse distance ties by hexagon ID. This measures projected straight-line distance from cell centroid to park geometry; centres inside parks have distance zero. It is not walking distance or entrance access.

The old feature searched only parks intersecting the 500 m grid context. Expanding to the complete available set corrects 110 hexagons, with a largest reduction of approximately 2,190.05 m. Park overlap features are unchanged.

Coverage reporting compares each nearest distance with the distance to the reconstructed extraction boundary and reports the margin. This is a geometric edge check, not proof of mapping completeness. The bbox extraction uses `complete_ways`, which does not guarantee complete multipolygon relations; OSM may omit or misclassify parks. Distances are described as nearest **available extracted mapped park**, never globally complete nearest public park.

## Feature contract and validation

The final schema separates identifier, placement proxy, 20 model feature columns and geometry. Commercial polygon-area features remain deferred. No target redefinition or model training occurs.

Checks enforce approved IDs, exact counts, positive/finite population inputs, nonnegative finite features, nesting, area bounds, CRS, geometry validity, baseline comparison by ID, and consistent CSV/GeoPackage/GeoJSON attributes. Absolute tolerance `1e-6` and relative tolerance `1e-10` permit floating-point area/intersection/serialization noise; integer counts must not drift. Candidates must pass before publication; immutable raw checksums are checked before and after execution.
